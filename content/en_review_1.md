## Fact fixes
- galaxy: h "literally means ‘milky way’" → "literally means ‘milky’"; m reworded. Greek galaxias (kyklos) is literally "milky (circle)"; "milky way" is the Latin via lactea calque, not the literal meaning of galaxy. KOREAN FIX: ‘우유의 길’ overstates the literal sense; suggest ‘우유 같은’ (or "원래 은하수를 가리켰다").
- pp-daughter: h "Latin lost the word that gives us daughter" → "Latin lost its own version of daughter". The old hook implied daughter came from a Latin word; daughter is Germanic, and Latin lost its own cognate.
- gasu: m now says 手 here means a skilled person ("like a deckhand"). The old line "歌 is song and 手 is hand" made the literal ‘singing hand’ read like the real meaning. KOREAN FIX: add that 手 means "someone skilled at something" (as in 選手, 運轉手).
- bd-pupil: removed the gloss "‘eye Buddha’" for nunbucheo; it isn't in the Korean.
- pp-niang: removed the gloss "a young lady" for nangja (娘子); it isn't in the Korean.
- bd-ventriloquism: "means exactly the same" → "means the same" (KO 뜻이 같아요; 術 = art/technique, so "exactly" overstated).
- bd-brainwash: "It's known as a literal rendering" → "It's said to be a literal rendering". Keeps the KO hedge (알려져 있어요) and fixes the "known as" (= called) misreading.
- bd-eolgul: UN-SKIPPED, for consistency with bd-mari (kept). Like mari, it's a native-Korean semantic shift with a cross-language parallel (Latin facies) that an English reader can follow. New h "Korean eolgul (face) originally meant ‘appearance’"; m follows the KO m (15th c. form/appearance → 17th c. face; facies first meant appearance); roman 얼굴 → eolgul. KOREAN FIX: hook ‘몸 전체’ (whole body) is stronger than its own m (형체, 모습). Suggest ‘형체’ or ‘모습’.
